# Face Lock v1: the raw laws

v1 is the face law with no learned model, as a working face lock with a public challenge: scan once, then only your
face opens it, lit by the screen. Measured 2026-10-27.

## Run it

```bash
python3 -I unlock_server.py                  # admin page: http://127.0.0.1:8777 (scan, unlock, try, delete)
MODE=challenge python3 -I unlock_server.py   # challenge page: http://127.0.0.1:8780 (try only)
cloudflared tunnel --url http://127.0.0.1:8780   # a public https link to the challenge page
```

The page is `webapp/index.html`. Runtime state lives in `data/webapp/` (ignored by git): the vault (the map as numbers
plus bars, never an image), the challenge counter and per-try log, the owner's own tries for diagnosis, and the challenge text.

## How it decides

1. **The light.** Every camera capture pairs a black-screen frame with a white-screen frame. White minus black is the face
   lit by the screen alone; the room's own light cancels, so scan and unlock see the same light anywhere.
2. **The scan.** A burst of 8 black/white pairs. The owner taps both pupils once; the first frame's map finds the eyes in
   the other frames. Up to 8 maps, each the law's direction codes at three scales (2,652 points).
3. **The bars.** For each map and each light × evidence cell: the best score among 14,192 lab-stranger tries made the
   way the app sees a face (Extended Yale B, 28 people, every light, 8 darkness levels, full photo, eye taps off by up to
   2%, the app's crop), plus 0.20. No cell's bar is lower than the front-light bar. The bars are authored as integer lanes.
4. **Liveness.** The page flashes black, white, top half, bottom half (random order), black, white. The screen's light
   must be at least 15% of the face's light (a phone screen showing a photo makes its own light), the top/bottom light
   ratio must keep 3D shape beyond a smooth fit (at least 0.12; flat photos are smooth), and the face must not move
   more than 1 px of the 48 × 42 frame.
5. **The answer.** OPEN if any map clears its bar. NOT NOW if the try is not live, the light differs from the scan, or a
   cell has too few lab tries to set a bar. Otherwise NOT YOU.

## Measured

| What | Result |
|---|---|
| Strangers never used for the bars (10 Yale B people, 5,120 tries made like live tries) | 0 opened; the closest was 0.33 below its bar; fewer than about 1 in 1,700 (rule of three; only 10 identities) |
| Before the fix (bars from 48 × 42 lab arrays, low side-light bars) | 11 of 228 through the app's path; fixed by measuring through the app's path and the front-light floor |
| Owner, live tries on the Mac | 3 of 3 opened (+0.79 to +2.72 above the bar) |
| Owner, map from the Mac webcam, try from the phone | opened; on the phone at a lower angle, 2 of 5 tries that passed liveness |
| Owner's iPhone photos from other days (front / head turned) | opened (+0.98 / +0.62) |
| Phone screen showing the owner's photo, held to the webcam | rejected: screen share 0.02, and 1.12 below the bar on the face law alone |
| Simulated flat print | rejected: 3D 0.04 |
| Public challenge, first evening | nobody but the owner opened it; the owner's mother got NOT YOU |
| Size and speed | the law's table is 512 integers; a map is 10.4 KB packed; one decision takes 7 ms on one CPU core, no GPU |

## Known limits

- The eyes are tapped: the automatic finders tried so far miss by 5–13% of the eye distance; the law needs about 4%.
- Angles: a map scanned on one camera at eye level opens less often from a phone held lower; scan on the device you use.
- Most public tries were stopped by liveness (moved, or too little screen light) before the face law judged them.
- The bars come from lab faces; the real-world false-accept rate is not yet established, and a real printed photo
  has not been tested.
- Scanning is heavy: measuring a new map's bars takes about 100 s on 3 CPU cores against about 11 MB of lab faces.

## Not in this repository

`data/` is ignored by git: the owner's photos and map, the challenge logs, Extended Yale B and CMU PIE (research-only
licences), and the 4 GB of dark-face model files.
