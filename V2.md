# Face Law v2: the NIST build

v2 is v1's law made automatic and exact, for NIST's FRTE 1:1 test. Nothing is tapped. Measured 2026-10-28 on
Extended Yale B, counted on one half of the people and tested on the other; the owner's own photos stay local.

## How it decides

1. **The face box.** OpenCV's frontal-face cascade (Viola-Jones), run by our own C++ evaluator. On the 38 Yale frontal
   photos and the owner's 12 images it finds the same faces as OpenCV (box overlap at least 0.986).
2. **The eyes (the eye law).** A table T[window offset, code] of counted log-odds that the 25 x 25 window around a
   point is an eye centre, over the law's codes (gradient sector x significance x brightness-ratio band). The best five
   centres per eye, a pair rule (one eye distance apart, level), two refinements. The weaker eye's score must reach
   the GATE (-6000), or there is no face: a false face found by the cascade scored -10,229; real eyes at least -3,356.
3. **The crop.** A similarity onto the canonical eyes of the 48 x 42 frame.
4. **The codes.** Weber (brightness-ratio) codes at three scales, 2,652 points: the ratio of the local second difference
   to the local brightness, so the light's gain cancels. The direction codes of v1 added nothing on top of them.
5. **The score.** The table FW (768 counted log-odds) summed over the codes, the best of 9 placements, averaged over
   both directions (each template carries both a map and probe codes).

The tables (FW, T), the quantisers and the gate are authored as integer lanes, proven over their whole input domains.

## Exactness and speed

- `v2ref.py` is the reference. Every float step is IEEE add/multiply/divide/sqrt in a fixed order. Kernels and the sRGB
  curve are frozen in `v2ops_tables.json`. scipy's own filters are not used, because its arm64 build fuses multiply-adds.
- The C++ port (`v2cpp/`) equals the reference bit for bit on 74 real photos at every stage. It also equals it on
  34 seeded checksums (`selftest`), which any machine must reproduce.
- One core, this Mac: 87 ms per 640 x 480 template (NIST limit 2,000 ms); 19 us per comparison (limit 100 us).

## NIST

`make_kaggle_bundle.py` builds `dist/facelaw_v2_nist.zip`: scrubbed sources plus a script that fetches NIST's 1:1 kit
and validation images, runs in an Ubuntu 24.04.3 container (NIST's required OS), self-tests, times, and runs NIST's
`run_validate_11.sh`. The archive it produces must be encrypted and signed by the developer and sent by them.

## Known limits

- Every measurement is on lab faces under changing light (38 people) and the owner's own photos. NIST's sets add age,
  pose, expression and other cameras, at false-match rates down to 1 in a million. v2 should be expected near the bottom
  of NIST's table.
- Cohort normalisation helps at the strictest thresholds, but it would ship lab faces in the library, so it is off.
